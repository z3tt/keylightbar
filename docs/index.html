KeylightBar 💡

Free & open source Elgato keylight control

Your Elgato Key Light, one click away in the macOS menu bar.

⬇︎ Download KeylightBar View on GitHub

What it does

🌑

Light off? One click on

Your light is off, you click, it's on — with your last-used preset. That's the whole idea.

💡

Light on? One click to presets

While it's on, the next click opens the preset menu: Meet, Work, Chill — or your own.

  • Presets

psst — this page acts as your light: click 🌑 to switch it on, 💡 to open the preset menu.

Why you'll keep it

🌗

Sleeps + wakes with your Mac

Off when your Mac sleeps, back on when you return — the wake half is what even Elgato's own app doesn't do.

🕑

Day and night on schedule

Bright light until 19:00, chill mode after — your presets switch by time of day, on their own.

🚀

Starts at login

Flip Start At Login in the 💡 menu and it's in your menu bar from the first boot — no Login Items fiddling in System Settings.

🫥

Invisible by design

No Dock icon, no companion windows, no dependencies. If it weren't for the 💡 in your menu bar, you'd forget it exists.

🎚️

Presets, built in-app

Seven sensible presets ship with it — Meet, Work, Bright, Cold, Warm, Night, Chill — and a tiny editor for your own. No config files, no terminal.

🔄

Always in sync

Change the light from Elgato's app or its physical controls — the icon keeps up with the real state, not just its own.

🔍

Zero setup

It finds your light by itself; on stubborn networks it scans for it anyway. And it never pretends to control a light it can't reach.

🤏

Tiny and private

One Swift file, zero dependencies, and it talks only to your light over your local network. Nothing leaves your house.

The real thing

Everything you just tried, on your Mac: control your light, define and schedule presets, all in a single menu bar icon. No Dock icon, no companion windows, no dependencies.

Get it

Works with every Elgato WiFi light on macOS. Free, open source, and ready in three steps.

  • Key Light ↗
  • Key Light Air ↗
  • Key Light Mini ↗
  • Ring Light ↗
  • Light Strip ↗
  1. Download KeylightBar.zip from the Releases page and unzip it.
  2. Open it once — macOS will ask (the app is unsigned). Approve via System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.
  3. Say yes if macOS asks for Local Network access — that's how KeylightBar reaches your light.
